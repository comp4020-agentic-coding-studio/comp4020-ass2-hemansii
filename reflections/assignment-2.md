# Assignment 2 — reflection

## What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

The breakthrough was realising that Claude was most useful when I stopped
treating its output as the answer and started treating it as something I had to
direct and judge. Early on, I was asking for ideas and content quite broadly,
and although it generated a lot, the course still felt vague. The turning point
was deciding that the course needed a clear final outcome: by the end of twelve
weeks, students should have built their own language.

Once that was fixed, I had a way to judge everything else. I could look at each
week and ask whether it helped students move towards that outcome. That made it
easier to reject weaker ideas, reorder topics, improve the assessments, and make
the course feel cumulative rather than like twelve unrelated lessons.

It also changed how I prompted Claude. Instead of asking it to "make this
better", I became more specific about what was wrong: the tone was too serious,
a section was too vague, the assessment weighting did not make sense, or the
language sounded unnatural. The quality improved once I could explain the
problem rather than just ask for another version.

## What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

This work made me want to be a developer who is better at directing and judging
AI-generated work, rather than simply producing more of it.

Claude could generate content and code quickly, but I still had to decide
whether the structure made sense, whether the writing sounded natural, and
whether the result actually matched the goal. I want to get better at setting
clear direction, giving useful constraints, and recognising weak output early.
