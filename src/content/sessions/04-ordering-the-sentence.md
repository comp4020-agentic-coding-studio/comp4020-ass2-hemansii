## Before the session

Watch the first season of *Game of Thrones*, or as much of it as you can stand.
You are listening for how much Dothraki you are given without subtitles, and
what the show expects you to pick up from context alone.

## In the session

The first hour is the interlinear gloss — three lines, the words, the morphemes,
the translation. It is the standard notation of the field and you will use it in
every remaining assessment in this course, so we do it properly once rather than
badly for nine weeks.

Then word order, chosen and written down. Then the two things people forget:
how you ask a question, and how you say no. Both feel trivial and neither is.
Some languages move the verb, some add a particle, some only change the
intonation, and a language that does all three inconsistently is a language you
have not designed.

## Afterwards

You now have sounds, words and an order to put them in, which means for the
first time you can write a sentence and somebody else can parse it.

Write one. Gloss it. Post it. It is the first thing in the semester that will be
legible to a stranger, and it is worth noticing that four weeks ago it did not
exist.
