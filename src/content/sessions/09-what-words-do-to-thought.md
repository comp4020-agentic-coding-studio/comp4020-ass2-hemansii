## Before the session

Watch *Arrival* again. You saw it in week two for its phonology and you will see
different things now, which is the point of screening it twice.

Read one of the papers from the essay brief in full, including the method
section. One paper read properly is more use than four abstracts.

## In the session

We begin by writing the film's argument on the board in the strongest form
anybody can manage. Arguing against a weakened version of the claim is easy and
proves nothing; the essay only works if you have first stated the case as well
as the film does.

Then thesis statements, out loud, one each. If yours can be restated as "the
film is unrealistic," it is not a thesis and you will be asked to try again. The
film is not a documentary and is not being assessed on accuracy. The question is
what it is claiming about the relationship between language and mind, and where
that claim comes from, and what it would take for it to be true.

## Afterwards

The essay is due at the end of next week. Bring a draft to the week ten
workshop if you want it read.

Then the course goes back to building. Two subsystems left, and both of them are
about what your language does not say.
