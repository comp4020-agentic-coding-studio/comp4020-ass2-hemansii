Wren visits once, for the orthography week, and stays for the workshop
afterwards.

They design typefaces, which is a trade with a much longer memory than most
people expect — the reason certain letters are shaped the way they are is
frequently that somebody was working fast with a broad-nibbed pen a thousand
years ago, and the shape survived the pen. Their contribution to week eight is
the part of script design that linguists tend to skip: what a letterform costs
to produce, at speed, by hand, with whatever tool your speakers actually had.

They are the reason the week eight workshop ends with everybody writing the same
sentence three times as fast as they can. Students find this a strange exercise
for about four minutes and then discover that two of their glyphs have collapsed
into each other.
