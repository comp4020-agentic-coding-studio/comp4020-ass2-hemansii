## The brief

> A language that works, and twelve lines of evidence that it does.

The crit is marked inside this submission rather than separately, because the
two halves are not separable. A document describing a language that cannot be
read aloud has not demonstrated a language, and a fluent performance of
something with no system behind it has not either.

The second criterion deserves reading twice. Thirty per cent of this assessment
turns on somebody else — a person from outside the course, unprepared, handed
one sentence of your text in your own script with no gloss and no pronunciation
guide. They will make mistakes. What is being marked is whether those mistakes
were avoidable: whether two of your letters are confusable, whether your
orthography under-specifies a vowel, whether your phonotactics permit a cluster
nobody can parse by eye.

This is why the course spends eight weeks on things that only matter out loud.

## What you submit

By the Friday after the crit — a single document containing the full
description, the lexicon, the script with a chart of its glyphs, and the text
with an interlinear gloss and a free translation.

Attach your week one design brief unchanged. The last criterion asks whether the
language you built is the one you set out to build, and you cannot answer that
without the original. Divergence is not penalised. Being unable to say where or
why it diverged is.
