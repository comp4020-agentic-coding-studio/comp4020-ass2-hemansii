The street language in *Blade Runner* — Cityspeak — is a mongrel of Japanese,
German, Hungarian and Spanish, and Edward James Olmos put much of it together
himself for Gaff rather than receiving it from the script. It occupies about
ninety seconds of screen time and it does more world-building than any
establishing shot in the film, because it tells you who arrived in that city,
in what order, and who has to accommodate whom.

That is what contact does to language. Where speakers of different languages
need to trade, a pidgin appears: reduced, nobody's first language, adequate for
the job. Where children grow up hearing that pidgin as their primary input, they
do something extraordinary with it — they supply the grammar it lacked, and
within a generation it is a creole, a full language with native speakers and
everything a full language has.

Your language currently has one register, which means it has no sociology.
Real speakers do not all sound alike. The same person will use four noticeably
different varieties before lunch depending on who they are talking to, and the
differences are not sloppiness; they are precisely governed and speakers notice
instantly when someone gets them wrong. So: who in your world speaks your
language as a second language? Who speaks the prestige variety, and what makes
it prestigious other than the fact that powerful people speak it?
