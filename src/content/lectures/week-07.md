The street language in *Blade Runner* — Cityspeak — is a mixture of Japanese,
German, Hungarian and Spanish, and Edward James Olmos put much of it together
himself for Gaff rather than receiving it from the script. It occupies about
ninety seconds of screen time and tells you more about the city than any
establishing shot does, because it shows who arrived there, in what order, and
who has to accommodate whom.

That is what contact does to language. Where speakers of different languages
need to trade, a pidgin appears: reduced, nobody's first language, adequate for
the job. Where children grow up hearing that pidgin as their primary input, they
do something extraordinary with it — they supply the grammar it lacked, and
within a generation it is a creole, a full language with native speakers and
everything a full language has.

Your language currently has one register, so it carries no information about
the society that speaks it. Real speakers do not all sound alike. The same
person will shift between several noticeably different varieties in a day,
depending on who they are talking to, and those shifts are not carelessness:
they are systematic, and speakers notice at once when someone uses the wrong
one. So: who in your world speaks your
language as a second language? Who speaks the prestige variety, and what makes
it prestigious other than the fact that powerful people speak it?

## Outline

- pidgin and creole, and what happens when children acquire one as a first language
- register: one speaker, several varieties, chosen by who is listening
- Olmos, Gaff, and ninety seconds of a language an actor built himself
- prestige, stigma, and whose variety your society treats as incorrect
