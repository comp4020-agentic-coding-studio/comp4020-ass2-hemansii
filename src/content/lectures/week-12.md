There is no film this week. The eleven compulsory screenings end at week
eleven, and this week is given over to your own work.

The session is not a presentation about your language; it is a demonstration of
the language in use. You read your text aloud, in front of people, at speaking
pace — and then a person who has never seen your orthography is handed one
sentence of it and asked to read it back. That second part is what the session
is really testing, and it is why the course has spent so much time on things
that only matter out loud: whether your phonemes stay distinct at speed,
whether your script survives being written quickly, whether a stranger can work
out how to pronounce it.

After that, questions. Any choice in your grammar may be questioned, and an
answer that appeals only to taste will not do. "It sounded better" is not a
defence. "The design brief said they were a maritime people who count in twelves,
so the numeral system is duodecimal" is.

Bring your week one brief. You will be handed it back and asked whether the
language you built is the one you set out to build. For most of you it will not
be, which is expected — but you should be able to say where it changed and
why.
