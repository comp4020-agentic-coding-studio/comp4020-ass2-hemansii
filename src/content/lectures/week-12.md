There is no film this week. Eleven weeks of compulsory screenings end at week
eleven, and this one belongs to you.

The session is not a presentation about your language. It is a use of it. You
read your text aloud, in front of people, at speaking pace — and then a person
who has never seen your orthography is handed one sentence of it and asked to
read it back. That second part is the real assessment, and it is why the course
has spent so much time on things that only matter out loud: whether your
phonemes are distinguishable at speed, whether your script survives being
written quickly, whether a stranger can get a foothold.

After that, questions. Any choice in your grammar is fair game, and the only
wrong answer is one that appeals to taste. "It sounded better" is not a
defence. "The design brief said they were a maritime people who count in twelves,
so the numeral system is duodecimal" is.

Bring your week one brief. You will be handed it back and asked whether the
language you built is the one you set out to build. Most of you will find it is
not, and that is not a failure — but you should be able to say when it diverged
and why.
